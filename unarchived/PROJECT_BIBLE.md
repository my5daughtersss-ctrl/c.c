### WINNING TOPIC SYSTEM (Oct 26 2026, owner-approved; supersedes topic rule #2)
**Method A: competitor outliers (data-driven).** Use only videos under 6 months old, sorted by most recent. An outlier is ≥3× its channel's median views (check absolute views too, because tiny channels inflate the multiplier). Then run the appearance check across the niche (NexLev outlier database plus YouTube search plus competitor channels):
- 1–3 real appearances, all outliers → copy the topic and tweak the title (new script, our own value).
- 3–10 channels, mostly outliers → do it fast.
- Outlier for one channel, average for others → **topic swap** only (same angle, new subject).
- Mostly flops → never. No one-hit wonders: a topic needs at least 2 independent outliers.
- Count copies in the last 30 days: rising is good, a flood is late.
- Check RPM and geography in NexLev (get_video_rpm, get_geography_revenue) before choosing.
**Method B: ICAM keyword search (needs human judgment).** Take 20 audience keywords and search real YouTube, excluding Shorts. Keep videos with 100K+ views, from channels under 100K subscribers, with views at least 5× subscribers; prefer mediocre thumbnails. A keyword with 3 or more passing videos is proven search demand. ⚠️ Keywords get polluted by fiction, mini-dramas and other meanings ("inheritance" etc.), so judge every result by hand. ICAM proves lasting demand, not trends.
**Scoring (make it at 6 or more out of 9):** passes A +3 · passes B +2 · copies rising but not flooded +1 · fits brand and File Lesson +1 · US, UK, Canada and Australia audience plus strong RPM +1 · mediocre competitor thumbnails +1.
**After launch:** check click rate and audience retention at 48 hours and 7 days; feed the results into the next topic choice. A trend can run as Part 1, 2, 3 while it lasts; drop it when it dies.
**Topic rule #2 ("no 50K+ video in the last 3–4 months") is superseded** by the appearance check.

## 5. STYLE DNA (from 4 Undertold transcripts: Genain quadruplets, Rose Williams, Lucia Joyce, Camille Claudel)
- **Hook (first 45–70 seconds):** a date and place scene → a contrast of 3–5 escalating short sentences → a famous-name anchor → a betrayal reveal → a mystery line → title drop: "This is the tragic story of [Full Name], [epithet]."
- **11-beat skeleton:** cold open (3%) → birth, era panorama, family sketches (8%) → innocence image, flagged "Hold on to that picture" (3%) → golden moment (10%) → planted motifs throughout → behind the façade (12%) → the descent with era context (15%) → the family decision plus a fair-witness pause (12%) → long aftermath, deaths in sequence, irony (15%) → second silencing and reclamation (10%) → bookend, 1–3 lessons, CTA (7%).
- **Rhythm:** long flowing sentence then a short punch; stacks of 3 fragments; anaphora in the finale; present-tense scene drops; parallel contrasts.
- **Tone:** compassionate, investigative, fair to everyone; admits uncertainty ("We don't know"); no gore.
- **Transitions:** time jumps; "But that was only the beginning"; callback hinges; context detours; "Let's pause here for a moment."
- **Retention:** open loops; motif callbacks every 5–8 minutes; chapter cliffhangers; "picture this" immersion; moral pauses; mini-reveals every ~3 minutes; the fair-witness twist; a bookend ending.
- **Added retention rules:** payoff preview in the first 30 seconds; stakes within 20 seconds; always 2 open loops; a re-hook every 90–120 seconds; **mid-video bomb at 40–55%**; today's-money conversions; real document quotes; no "finally" before the end; a next-file tease in the last 20 seconds.
- **Paragraph rule (Oct 26 2026):** every paragraph must reveal a new fact, change the meaning of an earlier fact, or explain a financial consequence. If it does none of these, cut it or rewrite it.
- **Direct address:** "you", "let's", rhetorical questions; CTA with a binary moral question plus "I read every single one."
- **Pace:** about 2.4–2.5 words per second (145–150 wpm). **Target about 4,800 words (±5%: 4,560–5,040), about 32–33 minutes.**
